## What earned its place

- **The soundboard gave the piano its body.** The wide-body trial made the geometry audible. The final structural board uses a roughly 2.05 × 1.37 m panel, with its outline, 17 rib positions and two bridge paths traced from the Model D drawing. The trace approximates the panel edge and omits the diagonal corner brace. Wood stiffness along and across the grain, rib stiffness, string loading and the yielding rim enter the vibration model. The listening setup uses a 35° open lid. [Boutillon, Ege and Paulello, Figure 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1210.3948)
- **The lowest bass needed a different string construction.** The preferred A0 has a 2.30 mm steel core inside its copper winding. At fixed outside diameter and speaking length, the model recalculates mass per unit length, tension at the target pitch and bending stiffness. This moves the upper partials relative to the fundamental. The core adjustment tapers across A0–E1 and rejoins the underlying scale at F1.
- **Strike position and touch had to be voiced together.** The near-15% strike was a useful listening discovery. The selected preset has a 12% base strike position, key-specific offsets and a shorter fraction in the top register. Strike location selects which modes the hammer excites. Nonlinear felt contact shapes their balance across velocities. Smooth hammer-speed and level curves let us check quiet treble without sacrificing the medium strike. Per-key partial and level trims remain part of the voicing.
- **Held treble needed its own adjustment.** We reduced string loss and eased coupling to the bridge on selected high notes so the tone kept body while the key was held. Small, unequal tuning offsets among the three strings on E5 and D6 shaped the beating. The rocking bridge and forced soundboard radiation remain active. Carrying the already-ringing state through a restrike fixed the click exposed by the Liszt opening.
- **The missing attack ring came from the other strings.** Hearing the ring last longer with sustain down, then recede when the pedal lifted, identified sympathetic speaking strings as the decisive listening mechanism. The rear string lengths also contribute. The selected bake solves vertical transfer between 88 bridge contacts using 1,621 board modes and the wave impedance of each string group. The existing finite strings provide ringing and damper response. One shared return gain of 1.42 gave 34% less isolated ring on D6 and 50% less on A5 than the original strong-coupling version over a two-second pedal-down hold.

## Reproducing the checkpoint

The Liszt recording uses the offline renderer with the balanced preset, per-key touch calibration, and baked bridge coefficients. The standalone player and plug-in load the instrument geometry and voicing, then use their own live MIDI velocity mapping and sympathetic-string response. Re-bake after changing string construction or board geometry. The semi-infinite string assumption describes the initial transfer. Later reflections between finite strings remain approximate. [Build the bank and render a MIDI performance](../tools/wave_grand/README.md)

For another instrument, choose the lid, microphones and room first. Compare bridge and microphone signals to locate a tonal problem, then check attack and held sound at soft, medium and hard strikes. Match note levels to hear timbre, retain common gain to judge register balance, and make the final decision in a pedaled phrase.
